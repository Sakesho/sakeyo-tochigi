Hot water, warm sake

A hot spring village two hours past Nikko, where you can taste sake in the shop before you buy it and warm the bottle in your own bath. One street, one liquor shop, and more open-air baths than houses.

Yunishigawa Onsen, Nikko · 14 min read
i
Getting there. Two trains and a bus, and why it takes that long.
ii
The village. A thousand people, more baths than houses, and one street to walk down.
iii
Honke Bankyū. Founded 1666. You cross a vine bridge to get to dinner. Every room faces the river.
iv
Yuzawaya Saketen. Ten seats in a liquor shop. Taste before you buy, and what to get.
v
Warming sake in the bath. Plus how not to annoy anyone else in the bath.
vi
Three more baths. Where to go next.
Warm it in the bath
Stand the sealed bottle in the tub while you soak. Private bath only — never a shared one. The water runs 42–45°, so the sake lands near 40°.
You don't need a thermometer. Cold sake smells of almost nothing. As it warms, the rice comes up out of the bottle. When you can smell it without leaning in, it's ready. Sharp or spirity means it went too far.

Matsuzaki Ken
Trained under a Living National Treasure, then dropped his own style and started again on 400-year-old Oribe. Makes sake cups and bowls. His work is in the Met and the V&A, and you can buy it in Mashiko.

Pickle maker · Nikko
Uwasawa Umetarō Shōten
Fourteen generations. His great-grandfather invented tamari-zuke after the war by pickling vegetables in the liquid that collects on ageing miso. Never heated, brought up from the cellar each morning, nothing sold more than three days old.

What changed from v1

  • Places is split. Eat & drink and Shop are different intents. Roughly half of what the research turned up is retail — bottle shops and ceramics galleries — and it had nowhere to live before.
  • Food is new. The page had no food block at all, which undercut the whole reason food is in this app.
  • Visit is new. Brewery experiences were invisible behind cards that report a sake count. Shimazaki's tunnel and Nishibori's tour are the two most bookable things in the prefecture.
  • Hero is an onsen stay. Yunishigawa: the village, a night at Honke Bankyū, the bottle shop, how to warm what you bought, and three more baths to go on to. One article that carries a town, an inn, a shop and a skill.
  • The warming scale sits below the hero as its own block. The teachable part lifted out so it works alone — someone who never opens the article still learns the four temperatures. Reusable on every prefecture page.
  • Bottle cards now match the app — dark shot, match ring, brewery and prefecture in caps, rule, sake profile. Section eyebrow carries the profile pair rather than the word "Catalog".
  • Artisans is now defined properly: people whose craft you eat, drink or drink from. Ceramic artist, pickle maker, yuba maker, butcher — and, to come, tofu maker, fishmonger, tea merchant, chef. The stone mason is gone; nobody drinks out of a wall.
  • Every artisan row leads to where you buy. The story is the person and the object, not the equipment.

Numbers

The stat row now reads 28 breweries, not 35. The Tochigi Sake Brewers Association lists 28 members across 14 municipalities. The catalog's 35 presumably includes non-members, dormant kura, or shochu-only producers — worth reconciling before an official asks, because they will.
