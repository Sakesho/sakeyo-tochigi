First impressions

The shop is in an old fertiliser merchant's warehouse built out of ōya stone, the same pale volcanic rock the sake warehouses in this prefecture are made of. The front room is bright. The stone room behind it is cooler and quieter, and that's where the good pieces are.

They opened in 1974 and have run over a thousand shows since. The exhibition changes every couple of weeks, so it's worth checking what's on before you go.

Nothing's behind glass. Everything is priced and on open shelves, and you're meant to pick it up.

What to buy

Around forty potters, nearly all from Mashiko. If you're only buying one thing, make it a guinomi — the small cup you drink sake from.

A guinomi from the sake section
The sake cups are all kept in one place instead of mixed in with the plates, so you can compare them. Thick and glazed for warm sake, thin for cold.
¥3,000–12,000
Wadagama, in the six traditional glazes
Persimmon, white, black, amber and two more. They carry enough of it that you can pick the glaze you want rather than take what is left.
¥2,500–8,000
A katakuchi
A lipped bowl you pour from. Better than a flask if you're drinking cold, and it stays out on the table rather than getting put away.
¥5,000–15,000
Whatever is in the gallery that fortnight
One potter at a time, and the only chance to see their whole range in one room. Check the dates before you book anything.

Ordering from abroad

They have an online shop with sake cups as their own category. Not everything in the building is listed — message them if you saw something in the shop and can't find it.

They pack pottery properly, which is the thing that actually matters. Allow two to three weeks.

Verdict

Come here before you visit any kilns. You'll see forty potters in half an hour and work out whose work you actually want.

Two decisions made here

  • One list, not two tabs. Sake and ceramics sit together with filter chips, because someone who came for a cup should run into a sake shop on the way.
  • Shipping is a tag on every row. Ships abroad, Ships JP, or nothing. Nine of the twelve post, which makes the section useful to someone who is not going to Japan.
Invented for the mockup: all prices, the hours, the card and English lines, the shipping times, and the description of the interior. Real: the 1974 opening in an 1898 fertiliser merchant's building, the ōya stone, 40+ potters, over 1,000 exhibitions, a show every two weeks, the Wadagama range in six traditional glazes, and the online sake-cup collection.
