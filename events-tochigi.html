Shimotsuke Tōji new sake release

Why this one

Most sake regions have an old guild that trains and certifies brewers. Tochigi had none, so in 2006 a group of young brewery workers built their own: a practical exam, a blind tasting and a written paper, run through the prefectural technology centre. Twenty-two people had passed by 2016.

This is the one night they all pour together, and the people behind the tables are the ones who made it. You can ask them anything, and they'll tell you.

Buy early. Tokyo sold out about three weeks ahead in 2026.

Before you go

Buy the day sales open
Early February, on e+. Nothing on the door.
Eat first
Two and a half hours, 26 tables, and barely any food.
Start at the far end
Everyone starts at the door and works right. The last tables are quiet for the first hour, so that's where you get to talk to people.
Ask who made it
Certified brewers wear a crimson jacket. If you want the person who actually made it rather than someone from the office, look for the jacket.

Why this one

Until 2024 the brewers' association ran a permanent tasting room in Utsunomiya called Sasara. It opened in 1999, weekday evenings only, two hours, ten glasses a person — kept small on purpose so it would not take trade from the bars nearby.

The building was falling apart, the association moved out, and Sasara closed. This replaced it: one day a year, twenty-four breweries, in a hall.

If you only get one day in Tochigi, make it this one. Nowhere else puts this many breweries in front of you at once.

Before you go

Take the afternoon session
The lunchtime session is busier. Either way you are out by six with time to eat.
Two dozen breweries is too many
Pick eight and do them properly. Four to start with are below.
Use the water
There's water on every table. Two hours of unlimited pours goes badly without it.
Don't buy here — buy after
Note what you liked and buy it at Shimadaya by the station instead. Same bottles, and you are not carrying them round the hall.

All dates are invented. They follow the real pattern — Mashiko at Golden Week and in November, the tōji release in Tokyo in spring and Osaka in May, shibori through Jan–Mar — but not one of them is confirmed. Replace before anything goes live, and do not put these in front of the prefecture as fact.

The template — nine blocks, in order

  • 1 Cover. One image, 390 × 224.
  • 2 Title. Date and time in crimson above the name, then a two-line standfirst saying plainly what it is.
  • 3 Actions. Primary is Tickets, or Directions if it's free. Secondary is always Add to trip.
  • 4 The basics. Fixed field list. Language is in there deliberately — it's the question a foreign visitor actually has.
  • 5 Why this one. Three paragraphs maximum. The reason to give up an evening.
  • 6 One image. People, not product.
  • 7 Before you go. Four practical things. Ideally at least one that only someone who has been would know — here, start at the far end of the room.
  • 8 What you'll find. Links out to breweries, makers or shops. This is where the event stops being a dead end.
  • 9 Make a weekend of it. Links to another event, a shop or a place nearby.

Dropping blocks

  • Shimotsukare has no tickets, no venue and no time. It keeps 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 and 8 — and 8 links to restaurants serving it rather than breweries.
  • Mashiko fair drops Tickets for Directions, and block 8 links to potters and galleries.
  • Shibori open days is a set of dates rather than one, so block 4 becomes a per-brewery list and block 3 loses the primary button.
