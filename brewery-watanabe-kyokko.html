Kyokkō

Watanabe Sake Brewery · 旭興

Kyokkō comes out of a hamlet called Susaki, up in the Yamizo mountains in the north-east corner of Tochigi. Ten minutes' drive and you're in Ibaraki. It is about as far from anywhere as a brewery in this prefecture gets.

They make around 700 koku a year and roughly nine tenths of it never leaves the area. Most of what they do goes into the ryokan at Kinugawa Onsen and the shops in Kurobane, which is why almost nobody outside Tochigi has drunk it — and why the ones who have tend to go on about it.

Water

Off a 1,022-metre mountain

The water is groundwater from the Mumo river, which rises on Mount Yamizo — 1,022 metres, the highest thing in this part of the country. It's medium-hard, which gives the sake structure rather than softness. The other half of the site is the cold: winters here drop below minus ten, and the brewing season runs through it.

Rice

Ōtawara grows more rice than anywhere in Tochigi

Local rice, almost entirely — Yamada Nishiki, Gohyakumangoku, Miyama Nishiki, Nihonbare, all grown nearby. In 2022 a daiginjo made from Yamada Nishiki grown in Sakuyama, a few minutes down the road, came first out of 312 entries at the Nanbu Tōji competition. It was the first time a sake made with Ōtawara rice had won it. There is also a bottle called Kurou, named after the old name for Kurobane, where the water, the rice and even the label all come from that one district — and only Kurobane shops are allowed to sell it.

Barely polished

Ninety-nine per cent of the grain, left on

Watanabe Hidenori is the fifth generation and does the brewing himself. Most breweries chase a lower polishing ratio; he went the other way. First a kimoto at 88% — barely milled — and then one at 99%, where only the bran has come off. There's a red-rice sparkling sake, a kijoshu, and Rosso: that 99% kimoto, aged in oak barrels, 11% alcohol, acidity of 6.0. None of it is normal and the orthodox bottles are excellent, which is the part that makes the rest worth taking seriously.

All three DNA points are sourced. Founded 1892 by an Echigo tōji working out of a rented farm barn; moved to the present site in 1912, east of the old one, hence the name — the sun rising in the east. Mumo river groundwater off Mount Yamizo, 1,022m, medium-hard. Winters below −10°C. Local rice; the Sakuyama Yamada Nishiki daiginjo took first place out of 312 at the 103rd Nanbu Tōji competition in 2022, the first win for Ōtawara rice. Kurou is Kurobane-only. Watanabe Hidenori, fifth generation, brews it. The 88% and 99% kimoto, Rosso at 11% and acidity 6.0 aged in oak, the red-rice sparkling and the kijoshu are all real products. ~700 koku, ~90% drunk locally, widely served in Kinugawa Onsen ryokan.

Why this brewery

  • It's on the mission itinerary — Day 3, 15:00, two hours.
  • Three genuinely distinct DNA points: a mountain water source, a rice story with a national win attached, and a brewer doing something nobody else does.
  • Almost nothing about it exists in English.
